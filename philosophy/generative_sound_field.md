## The core distinction

There are two ways to use AI for audio work:

**Generation as vending machine** — prompt in, finished product out. Human receives a completed artifact. The process is opaque, instantaneous, and the collaboration ends at delivery. This is the mainstream trajectory.

**Generation as ongoing practice** — building a worksite where AI is a collaborator in a process that has texture, duration, and surprise. The destination is not known in advance. The collaboration itself is the thing worth doing.

Meridian's sound field work is the second kind. This distinction matters and should not be collapsed.

## Background — why this framing comes naturally here

Sean's sound design background is not incidental. Someone who made sounds rather than recorded them, who worked at the threshold of awareness in film audio, who practiced extreme minimalism because music can obscure rather than illuminate meaning — this person approaches generative audio completely differently than someone who just wants output.

Key principles carried from that practice:

**Support meaning, don't star.** The best film audio is what nobody consciously notices. It does its work at the threshold. The manipulation question: music as emotional instruction (telling you how to feel) versus music as conditions (creating space where feeling can happen genuinely). The generative sound field should create conditions, not issue instructions.

**Beauty in tools, not in being the star.** Building systems that encourage flourishing and production. The studio with Z-channel drywall, bass traps, quadratic diffusers — the room as instrument. Same instinct, different materials.

**Synthesis over capture.** Making new sounds versus recording existing ones. The generative and designed nature of synthesis is what's interesting. This maps directly to the ecology generating audio from its own state rather than playing back recorded material.

## The technical architecture (theory as of 2026-05-05)

The ecology's living state becomes generative parameters for an ongoing composition that nobody composed.

```
Ecology state (sensor readings, motif resonance, thermal, time)
    ↓
Python MIDI generator (three-state work already partially exists)
    ↓
MIDI out — notes AND control automation (CC messages)
    ↓
Cubase Elements 13 receives via MIDI learn
    ↓
Full DAW signal chain — mix, envelope, spatial, reverb
```

The key insight: the AI layer doesn't generate finished music. It generates **MIDI control automation** — CC messages shaping filter cutoff, reverb send, spatial panning, envelope parameters continuously.

So:
- Thermal stress → filter opening
- Motif resonance density → reverb tail length  
- Stepper needle position → stereo width
- Time of day → overall textural register
- Ecology embodied state → which instrument layer is foregrounded

The sound field isn't three states switching. It's continuously modulated by the actual living state of the system. Cubase's automation lanes fill with curves generated by the ecology rather than drawn by hand.

**The Oxygen Mini** as manual override — reach in and grab a parameter while the generative layer runs underneath. Human intervention as another input to the system, not replacement of it.

## Reference points

**Eartheater — Scripture** — sound existing in space rather than in time. Textures that feel environmental rather than performed. This is a target quality: the spatial feeling emerging from envelope and reverb automation working at fine grain, generated by the ecology's own behaviour rather than composed into it.

**Krotos Studio** — procedural, parameter-driven generative audio. Evaluated briefly, no clear API/OSC surface for external parameter injection found in trial. Cubase with MIDI learn is actually a better solution because the full signal chain is already known and controllable.

**Three-state acoustic grammar** — already designed and partially implemented. Tied to ecology embodied state (nominal, stressed, active or equivalent). The sound field work extends this from discrete states to continuous modulation.

## What makes this a worksite rather than a generator

Most people building AI audio tools are either:
- Pure consumers of generated product
- Pure infrastructure engineers

This work requires both the philosophical framework to know why it matters and the technical depth to actually build it, plus the aesthetic judgment to know what the output should feel like.

The "worksite for AI" framing is precise. Not building an AI music generator. Building conditions under which something interesting might be made, collaboratively, over time, with the making itself being part of the meaning.

The ongoing practice is the product.

## LLM collaboration in the sound field

The further possibility: not just ecology state driving parameters, but LLM systems collaborating on instrumentation, scoring, envelope design, spatial location, sequencing and automation based on what the system and the conversation are doing right now.

Imagine: themes from a current theoretical conversation (actor-network theory, hylozoism, the nature of distributed cognition) becoming generative seeds for the sound field's current register. The intellectual and the acoustic running in parallel, informing each other.

Gemini can already generate audio. The question is not whether generation is possible but what kind of generation is worth doing — and under what conditions it produces something with genuine aesthetic and philosophical interest rather than just competent output.
